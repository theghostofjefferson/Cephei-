A student physics and astronomy group

Free stargazing nights and physics workshops, open to anyone.

We run overnight stargazing camps, hands-on physics workshops, and small pop-up telescope stands in parks and school yards. You don't need any math background to come.

Join us Try the simulations

So far we have had over 1,200 students at 18 camps and star nights. Everything is free.

Namesake star 887 light-years away

Delta Cephei pulses every 5.4 days. That rhythm is tied to its true brightness, which is how astronomers use stars like it to measure distances to other galaxies.

Three kinds of sessions

Dark-sky camps

Overnight telescope sessions away from city lights. We cover finding your way around the sky and taking photos with a phone, and there is a campfire at the end.

Build-it workshops

Afternoon labs where you build something and take it home: a spectroscope from a CD, a water rocket, or a small orbit simulation in code.

Pop-up science

Solar telescopes and tabletop physics demos set up in school yards, parks and plazas. Walk up whenever you like.

Cepheid variables

Cepheid stars swell and shrink on a regular cycle. The longer the cycle, the brighter the star really is, which is how we use them to measure distances.
